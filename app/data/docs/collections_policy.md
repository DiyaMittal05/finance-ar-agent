# Collections Policy (synthetic)

This policy governs how the accounts receivable team, and any automated assistant acting for it, follows up on unpaid invoices. Days overdue are counted from the invoice due date.

## Dunning stages

### Stage 1: 1–30 days overdue
Send a **friendly reminder** email. Assume the delay is an oversight. Include the invoice number, amount outstanding and original due date, and offer a copy of the invoice.

### Stage 2: 31–60 days overdue
Send a **firm reminder**. Ask the customer to confirm a payment date within 5 business days. Mention that late fees apply under their terms, if their terms include late fees.

### Stage 3: 61–90 days overdue
Send a **final notice**. State that the account may be placed on **credit hold**, which pauses new orders, if payment or a payment plan is not agreed within 10 business days. Copy the Finance Manager.

### Stage 4: more than 90 days overdue
**Do not send further automated reminders.** Escalate the account to the Collections Manager for a phone call and a decision on next steps. Only the CFO can approve referral to an external collections agency or legal action.

When one email covers several invoices, use the stage of the **most overdue** invoice, unless that invoice is in Stage 4, in which case escalate and do not email.

## Disputed invoices
Pause dunning for any invoice with an open dispute. Acknowledge the dispute, state that the AR team is reviewing it, and do not request payment of the disputed amount or apply late fees to it. Undisputed invoices for the same customer follow the normal stages.

## Data quality
If an invoice is missing its due date (it has **no due date** in the ERP), do not state a days-overdue figure for it and do not include it in dunning. Flag it to the AR team so the master data can be corrected.

If a customer name matches more than one account, confirm which account is meant before quoting balances or drafting emails.

## Prohibited in customer communication
- Threatening legal action, collections agencies or credit reporting in any automated email.
- Adding penalties, interest or fees that are not in the payment terms or the contract.
- Sharing information about other customers.
- Offering discounts, write-offs or payment plans that a Finance Manager has not approved.
- Confirming that an invoice is paid unless the ERP shows the payment.

## Tone
Professional, concise and courteous. Use the customer's AP contact by name when one is on file.
